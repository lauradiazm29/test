    Methodology, coverage and sources

    What this application shows

    Four indicator modules computed from open data published by the Spanish Ministry of the Interior and by the National Statistics Institute (INE). Every number displayed here comes from a table built by sql/indicators.sql and exported by the notebook notebooks/04_indicators.ipynb. Nothing is computed in the browser except the class breaks of the map and, in module A, the weighted index, both of which depend on choices the visitor makes and so cannot be precomputed.

    Coverage: the most important limitation

    The Ministry of the Interior publishes municipal crime data only for municipalities above a population threshold. The Community of Madrid has 179 municipalities and only a minority of them appear in the source.

    Municipalities without published data are drawn on the map in flat grey with a dashed outline and are excluded from the colour scale. They are never treated as zero: absence of data is a property of the source, not a measurement.

    Why the map looks the way it does

    The map draws the municipal boundaries and nothing else. There is no relief, no roads and no place names underneath, because none of them carries information about the indicator and all of them compete with the colour the map is there to communicate. The only geography that matters for this data is which municipality a point belongs to, and the boundary lines say that completely.

    Data published one figure per municipality can honestly be drawn in a limited number of ways. This page uses a choropleth, which fills each municipality with a colour taken from a scale. A heat map in the strict sense, the smooth cloud of colour that most people picture, requires one point per event; the Ministry publishes municipal totals and no coordinates, so a heat map here would be an invention rather than a visualisation. A cartogram, which distorts area to match the value, was rejected because it makes the region unrecognisable for a reader who does not already know it well.

    The colour classes are quantiles: each colour holds the same number of municipalities. The alternative, giving each colour the same range of values, would place almost every municipality in the lowest class, because the municipality of Madrid is an order of magnitude above every other one on most indicators. The price of quantiles is that colour then encodes position in the ranking rather than magnitude, which is why the bar chart below the map, where lengths are comparable, is the view to use when magnitude matters.

    Notes on individual indicators

    • Module A. The rate uses the resident population of the municipality as the denominator, so it does not correct for commuting, tourism or any other daytime population. Municipalities that attract many non residents therefore appear worse than a per capita reading would justify.
    • Module B. Recorded and cleared offences refer to the same reporting period but not necessarily to the same offences, since an offence recorded in one year can be cleared in the next. This produces a clearance rate above one in a small number of categories. Those rows are flagged rather than hidden.
    • Module C. Victims are recorded from age zero, arrested or investigated persons from age fourteen, which is the age of criminal liability under Organic Law 5/2000. The two distributions are therefore only comparable over the five age bands from 14 to 17 upwards. Victim records also include an unknown sex category with no population denominator, and the rate per 10,000 inhabitants is defined only for the total over age bands, because the population is available broken down by sex and not by age band.
    • Module D. Between 37 and 79 per cent of recorded offences fall in the residual category "other offences", which mixes violent and non violent conduct and cannot be assigned to either class. The violent share is therefore computed over the classified offences only, and the unclassified share is displayed next to it so that the reader can judge how much of the crime mix the ratio leaves out.
